The IELTS Speaking Test
The IELTS Speaking test is a face-to-face interview with a certified examiner and lasts between 11 and 14 minutes. It is recorded so that the result can be checked later.
The test has three parts. In Part 1, which lasts four to five minutes, the examiner asks general questions about familiar topics such as home, family, work and hobbies.
In Part 2 the candidate receives a task card with a topic and has one minute to prepare notes. The candidate then speaks for one to two minutes without interruption. The examiner stops the candidate after two minutes.
Part 3 is a discussion of about four to five minutes. The questions are more abstract and are connected to the topic in Part 2, for example asking the candidate to compare past and present or to predict the future.
Four criteria are used for marking, and each is worth 25 percent of the score: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation. Scores are given in bands from 0 to 9, and half bands such as 6.5 are allowed.
Candidates are advised not to memorise answers, because examiners are trained to notice rehearsed language and it lowers the fluency score. It is better to extend answers with a reason and an example. If a candidate does not understand a question, they may ask the examiner to repeat it, and this does not reduce the score.
The speaking test can take place on the same day as the other sections or up to seven days before or after them.
