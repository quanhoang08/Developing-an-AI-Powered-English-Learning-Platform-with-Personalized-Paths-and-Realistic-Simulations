The Shadowing Technique
Shadowing is a language learning method in which the learner listens to a recording and repeats it aloud almost at the same time, staying only about one second behind the speaker. The method was popularised by the interpreter and professor Alexander Arguelles.
The technique trains pronunciation, rhythm and intonation rather than vocabulary. Because the learner must copy the speaker's stress and pauses, the mouth muscles become used to the sounds of the language.
There are two main forms. In no-text shadowing, the learner listens and repeats without reading anything. In text shadowing, the learner follows a transcript while speaking, which is easier and is recommended for beginners.
A typical session has four steps. First, listen to the audio once to understand the general meaning. Second, read the transcript and look up unknown words. Third, shadow the audio while reading the text. Fourth, shadow again without the text and record your own voice to compare it with the original.
Experts advise choosing audio that is slightly below the learner's listening level, at about 90 percent comprehension, so that attention can go to the sounds. Clips of one to two minutes are long enough to be useful and short enough to repeat many times.
Ten to fifteen minutes of daily practice is enough. Longer sessions cause fatigue, and tired learners tend to copy the sounds carelessly.
Shadowing does not replace conversation. It improves accuracy of sound, but the learner still needs to speak with real people to practise choosing words quickly.
